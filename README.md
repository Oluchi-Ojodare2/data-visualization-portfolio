In Flex 3, I calculated Profit Margin % separately for each tier by dividing total profit by total revenue in my PivotTable. In Power BI, I would build this as a measure because the margin should change based on what the user is viewing. Instead of averaging the margin for individual orders, the measure recalculates total profit divided by total revenue for the selected tier, region, or discount band. This gives Marcus the correct margin for whatever filters he applies without needing me to create a new PivotTable each time.

Introduction to Data Visualization in Power BI, completed on 10/10/2026: [a link to my published Tableau story](https://public.tableau.com/views/PowerBICertifications_17905809882230/PowerBIStory_1?:language=en-US&publish=yes&:sid=&:redirect=auth&:display_count=n&:origin=viz_share_link).
In Flex 3, I built an Account ScatterPlot comparing total revenue with net contributions across customer accounts. I would rebuild this view in Power BI and use conditional formatting to highlight accounts with strong revenue but weak net contributions. This would help Marcus identify accounts that generate sales but may not contribute proportionately to profitability, directing attention toward pricing, discounts, or costs. I also created a Profit by Discount Band view, grouping discounts into ranges using calculated fields. Rebuilding it with interactive filters and clear visual indicators would make it easier to compare profitability across discount levels and investigate where discounting may be undermining returns.
At Southwest, I would use Excel for quick reconciliations, one-off calculations, and flexible data checks; Tableau when exploratory visual analysis and polished storytelling are the priority; and Power BI when connected data models, reusable DAX measures, and interactive reporting are needed consistently across teams. My DataCamp Data Visualization in Power BI course is helping me develop these reporting skills. My main gap is that I have not yet published and maintained a Power BI report for real business users. I would address this by publishing a practice report to Power BI Service, testing scheduled refreshes and access permissions, and incorporating user feedback before taking responsibility for a business-critical dashboard.
